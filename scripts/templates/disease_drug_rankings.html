How to read this page
  • Reversal (rev): how strongly the drug pushes the cohort's changed regulators back toward normal tissue, with strongly changed regulators counting more. Specificity (spec): reversal for this cohort minus the drug's average reversal for cancers of other tissues. Mechanism specificity (spec_mech): specificity after removing the part explained by slowing cell division; this is the default ranking (D80). For subgroups, vs sibling (spec_sib_mech) compares the subgroup with its sibling subgroups and is their default ranking. Big numbers are percentiles within the cohort; small numbers are the raw scores.
  • Drugs: only LINCS compounds matched to an FDA-approved ingredient in Drugs@FDA (fetched 2026-10-29; marketed = a current prescription or OTC product, discontinued = approved, no current product). Where several LINCS compounds are the same ingredient, the one scoring best on the cohort's lead score is shown, as on the FDA rankings page. Unlike that page, weak-evidence and non-reversing drugs are kept here (weak evidence hidden by default).
  • Rank is the drug's position among all FDA drugs of the cohort on the chosen score, before any filter.
  • Target absent (D102c): a drug whose known targets (ChEMBL mechanism, else strong binding) are barely made by this cohort's typical tumor (median under 1 TPM or under 10% of a typical high level across cancers) is ranked below every other drug and marked Target absent (hidden with the weak-evidence drugs). Minority target: at least 3 tumors in the cohort make the target at a high level, e.g. ALK in lung adenocarcinoma, so a biomarker-selected subset may still benefit. Rankings describe the cohort's typical tumor.
  • Main target (D105b, FDA drugs): the ChEMBL curated mechanism; else annotated targets confirmed by binding data at family-specific potency cutoffs (kinases 30 nM, GPCRs and nuclear receptors 100 nM, ion channels 10 uM, others 1 uM); else annotated targets, marked Unverified binding. Drugs acting on DNA or on pathogens are not checked against human proteins. Secondary targets list other strong binders (median of Ki / Kd / IC50 / EC50 values).
  • Receptor absent in tested lines (D104c): the drug acts only through receptors, and none of them is made (TPM under 1) by the cell lines its score came from, nor raised by the drug there; the score cannot reflect its mechanism, so it ranks just above the target-absent drugs. Acts on tumor environment: the receptor is missing from the lines but tracks the tumor's stroma, immune or blood-vessel content (e.g. VEGF receptors in kidney cancer), so its absence from cancer lines is expected; not demoted.
  • Timing (D100): drugs with both 6 h and 24 h LINCS profiles are also ranked on each time point. Early + late reverses the disease in both; early only is a direct effect the cell adapts to; late only is a slower or secondary effect. The main ranking uses all time points, because known uses track the 24 h response better (D100).
  • Weak-evidence drugs are hidden by default: unmatched lines (the drug's best tested line looks nothing like the cohort) and low transfer (a borrowed drug whose effect carries over poorly between lines, confidence below 0.5) not reproduced on 2nd plate (D109a: every active measurement comes from one compound plate, and a second plate testing the same doses saw nothing), no effect in matched line (D109: a borrowed score for a drug that was measured in this cancer's own matched cell line and did nothing there), above patient exposure (D109: the lowest active lab dose is more than 10 times the peak blood level in the FDA label) and local use only (D107: every FDA-approved form of the drug is local, such as a cream, eye drop or inhaler, so it cannot reach a tumor inside the body; bladder instillation counts for bladder cancer). Other flags are shown but not hidden: multi-target (more than 5 annotated targets), proliferation-driven (the score comes mostly from slowing division), generic mechanism (D109: blocks core cell machinery: protein synthesis, the sodium pump or RNA polymerase, so it changes almost every regulator at once) and few regulators.
  • Receptors: the drug's annotated receptor targets expressed in this cohort's tumors, after the receptor evidence gate. Green: ChEMBL supports binding (curated mechanism, or affinity 1 µM or better; 100 nM for kinases) and the receptor is present in at least half of the cell lines this cohort's score used. Amber: it binds, but the receptor (or the full receptor complex) is missing from those lines or from the tumors, so the score cannot come through it. Struck out: no binding evidence in ChEMBL. 1 line marks a pass resting on a single cell line. The window is how many log10 units weaker the drug binds this receptor than its strongest measured target (0 = its main target).
  • Literature (rerun 2026-11-06 on the current rankings): every drug row is searched by its FDA name (PubMed: the drug and the disease in a title or abstract; ClinicalTrials.gov: drug as intervention, disease as condition), plus a LINCS-name search of each disease's top 10 by mechanism specificity and by reversal. A hit means the pair has been studied, not that the drug worked: failed trials and negative papers count too. ClinicalTrials.gov matches loosely, so trial counts are an upper bound.
  • Scores are computational predictions. Checks so far (C28, C29, D99): drugs move their target pathways as expected in 75% of profiles, but the ranking recovers known uses only modestly and does not predict which cell lines a drug kills (PRISM / GDSC). Treat rankings as hypotheses.
