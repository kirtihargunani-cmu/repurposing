How to read this page
  • Compounds are LINCS compounds that pass the target check and were tested in this cohort's own cell lines (no unmatched-lines flag). Rank is the compound's position among all such compounds of the cohort on mechanism specificity (subgroups: vs sibling), before filters. Only compounds with at least one close FDA drug are listed.
  • Close means the compound and the FDA drug share at least one annotated target (drug-metabolism genes excluded) and change regulator activity the same way: mean correlation of their line-specific regulator activity of at least over at least cell lines where both were profiled. The number shown is that correlation.
  • For each FDA drug: its own percentile in this cohort and how it was measured. Borrowed means it was never tested in this cohort's tissue; needs stand-in marks borrowed or weak-evidence drugs, for which this compound's score is the closest measurement in the cohort's own lines; with this compound is the average of the drug's own percentile and this compound's, which tracked the drug's measured rank better than either alone. Literature counts are PubMed papers naming the drug and the disease, and ClinicalTrials.gov matches.
  • The check above: for FDA drugs that were tested in their cohort's lines, the best stand-in was chosen with similarity measured outside those lines, and its percentile compared with the drug's own. The method was adopted only because it beat random target-sharing compounds by a margin fixed beforehand. A stand-in is a second opinion, not a replacement for testing the drug.
  • Scores are computational predictions, not yet validated against measured drug sensitivity (PRISM / GDSC).
