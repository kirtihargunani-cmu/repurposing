1 · The yardstick

A good ranking puts each cancer’s approved drugs near the top

  • Repurposing Hub (Broad Institute): a curated list of launched drugs with their approved indications. Strict, so few labels: 107 approved cancer × drug pairs in our drugs.
  • ChEMBL (EMBL-EBI): drug indications gathered from labels, regulators and late-stage trials (phase 3 or approved). Broader, so more labels: 336 pairs. The two lists overlap heavily.
  • A pair counts as known use when an indication names that cancer (or its subtype) and a cancer word.
  • The test compares, inside each cancer, every approved cancer drug with every other cancer drug.
AUC = Σcancers Uc / Σcancers (approvedc × othersc) = share of approved-vs-other pairs where the approved drug ranks higher (0.5 = chance)
2 · Where we started

The reversal score asked the wrong question

It scored whether a drug’s lab gene-expression signature undoes the tumor’s signature, measured as regulator activity.

rev = − Σi wi · ai / √(Σ wi²) w = tumor-vs-normal regulator activity (NES), a = drug’s effect on regulator i spec = z(rev in this cancer) − mean z(rev in cancers of other tissues) spec_mech = spec after removing proliferation-linked regulators → ranked within each cancer
  • Result: 0.46 (Hub) and 0.43 (ChEMBL), at or below chance. Fixing seven scoring errors left both unchanged.
  • Tumors share most of their signature (growth, proliferation), so the same broad drugs reverse every cancer.
  • The signature comes from cell lines that often match the cancer poorly, and is measured once per line.
3 · Filtering

Four checks remove a drug; lab-signature checks became flags

Bars to scale. The 1,112 pairs that pass the checks but do not act on a protein (for example alkylating chemotherapy) are listed separately. The old rules also removed drugs for lab-signature reasons, which is why they kept less than half as many.

Removes about the patient and the target

  • Target not made in the cancer
  • Main target missing in the typical tumor
  • Only topical, inhaled or eye forms
  • Lab dose above what blood reaches

Flags about the lab signature

  • Cell lines poorly matched or borrowed
  • Effect not reproduced
  • No effect on the cancer’s own cells (warning)
  • Signature does not reverse the tumor’s

5 · How the score works

Target role, weighted by how strongly the drug binds

Layer 1 · does the gene matter in this cancer?

D = Σ w·(−z) / Σ w A = f − median(f over cancers) O = regulator NES, or log2 fold change vs normal T = mean(D, A, O) each as a percentile 0–100
  • D: CRISPR knockout hurts the matched cell lines (z) more than cancer lines in general; w = how well each line matches
  • A: share f of tumors with a recurrent activating mutation or amplification
  • O: turned up in the tumor

Layer 2 · how does the drug engage it?

r = 10^(p − p_best) E = 50 + r · (T − 50) S = mean of E over the drug’s targets lead = percentile of S in the cancer
  • p: measured potency (pChEMBL); the strongest target has r = 1
  • Agonists flip D and O; a gene family or complex counts once
  • 75% of targets have a measured potency; the rest count as r = 1
  • Binding does not change accuracy (with vs without it, held out: 0.64 vs 0.66 on Hub, 0.62 vs 0.61 on ChEMBL). We keep it so a weak binder never gets full credit for a target.
  • Occupancy at patients’ free blood level, Cmax·fu / (Cmax·fu + Kd), is shown for 86 drugs (24 below 50%). It is not scored, because it did not improve the test either.
6 · Sanity check

Anti-estrogens moved from wrong to right

Median within-cancer percentile of fulvestrant, tamoxifen and raloxifene: luminal A 35 → 77, luminal B 37 → 66, basal 67 → 17. The estrogen receptor drives it: luminal lines depend on it and it is active in luminal tumors.

7 · Still open

Three questions before the lists are acted on

Chemotherapy targets

  • Genes every cell needs (tubulin, TOP2A) carry no cancer-specific signal; where chemotherapy is approved tracks how fast the cancer divides.
  • Scoring them on proliferation raised the test to 0.67 / 0.65.
  • But every such drug in a cancer then ties, and statins and cyclosporine count as “essential” in cell lines. Needs a curated target list or a tiebreak before release.

Minority alterations

  • EGFR is mutated in about 13% of lung adenocarcinomas, yet scores near the top for all of them.
  • 74 of 410 top-10 rows rest on a change most tumors lack, against the typical-tumor rule.
  • Options: count only common alterations, weight by share, or list biomarker-defined drugs separately.

Drugs that share a target

  • Drugs on the same target score alike, so a top 10 can fill with one class (glucocorticoids, statins).
  • Decision pending on whether to keep one or two per class.

Independent validation is still missing: cell-line killing data (PRISM, GDSC) show no signal for either score, and the approved-drug test is the only one that moved.
