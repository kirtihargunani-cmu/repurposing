From reversal to target role

Replacing a signature-reversal objective with a three-layered scoring objective, grounded in target biology and drug–target engagement, substantially improved concordance with established clinical indications.

1 · Evaluation framework

Concordance with approved indications serves as the reference standard

  • Drug Repurposing Hub (Broad Institute): curated approved indications of marketed drugs; a conservative reference (107 cancer–drug pairs).
  • ChEMBL (EMBL-EBI): indications compiled from regulatory labels and phase III trials; a broader reference (336 pairs) that largely subsumes the Hub.
  • Concordance is assessed within each cancer, so cancer-wide differences in drug activity do not contribute.
AUC = Σcancers Uc / Σcancers (approvedc × otherc) probability that an approved agent outranks another anticancer agent
2 · Baseline

Transcriptional reversal did not recover established indications

rev = − Σi wi · ai / √(Σ wi²) w = tumour regulator activity, a = drug-induced regulator activity spec = z(rev, this cancer) − mean z(rev, cancers of other tissues), proliferation-linked regulators excluded
  • Concordance was at or below chance (0.46 / 0.43) and was insensitive to correction of seven implementation errors, indicating a limitation of the objective rather than of its implementation; section 3 shows it is not a limitation of the data either.
  • Tumour signatures are dominated by programmes shared across cancers, so broadly acting compounds reverse most cancers alike.
  • Drug signatures derive from cell lines that are often poor surrogates for the tumour of interest.
3 · Measurement ceiling

Reversal fails because of the objective, not the reproducibility of the data

  • Reliability. Each drug was scored twice from independent experiments in the same cell line (a different dose or time point), counting only drugs whose every experiment was replaced. The two within-cancer rankings agree at a median Spearman r = 0.80 across the 41 cancers (0.43–0.91; lowest where scores rest on a single matched line, e.g. prostate, melanoma, breast).
  • Ceiling. By the classical attenuation bound, a score measured at this reliability can reach at most AUC 0.95 against the approved indications.
  • Attainment. The reversal objective attains essentially none of that signal: −0.09 / −0.16 of the attainable signal at 0.46 / 0.43, and +0.03 / −0.04 at 0.51 / 0.48 on the drug set of the three-layered objective (Hub / ChEMBL).
  • The three-layered objective does not use the transcriptional (LINCS) data, so this ceiling does not apply to it.
D = 2 · AUC − 1 Somers’ D, the rank-correlation form of the AUC AUC_max = (1 + √r) / 2 ceiling at reliability r attainment = D / √r share of the attainable signal

The ceiling treats approved indications as error-free and carries a correlation bound over to a rank statistic, so it is an upper bound rather than an achievable target. Counting dose and time differences as noise lowers r and therefore the ceiling, so the true attainment of reversal is, if anything, lower. A stricter split-half estimate on the most replicated drugs is in progress; the figures are provisional.

4 · Eligibility criteria

Exclusion is reserved for pharmacological and biological implausibility

Bars drawn to scale. Agents without a protein target are reported separately (1,112 pairs). Prior criteria additionally excluded drugs for reasons of signature quality, which removed most candidates.

Exclude implausible in patients

  • Target not expressed, or absent in the typical tumour
  • No systemic formulation
  • Effective concentration above attainable plasma levels

Annotate uncertainty in the cell-line data

  • Poorly matched or borrowed cell lines
  • Effect not reproduced across replicates
  • No activity in the cancer’s own cell lines
5 · Development of the objective

Each revision targeted a specific failure of the previous objective

Transcriptional reversal

0.46 / 0.43 · captured pan-cancer programmes rather than cancer-specific biology

Target role in the cancer

0.67 / 0.62 · large gain, but scoring by each drug’s best target favoured promiscuous compounds

Target engagement

0.64 / 0.62 · target families counted once and weighted by binding affinity, removing the multi-target bias

Learned component weights

Weights fitted on one reference failed to generalise to the other; equal weighting was retained and reversal received zero weight

Three-layered scoring objective

0.62 / 0.63 vs 0.49 / 0.48 for reversal on the identical drug set; applied to cancers and molecular subgroups

Proliferation-weighted scoring of essential targets

0.67 / 0.65 · cytotoxic targets scored by tumour proliferation, weighted by each target’s coupling to it; current implementation

Each stage was evaluated on its own eligible drug set (the engagement-stage ChEMBL value excludes Hub-labelled pairs), so the trend is informative and small differences are not. The paired comparison on an identical drug set is 0.62 / 0.63 versus 0.49 / 0.48.

6 · The three-layered scoring objective

Target relevance, modulated by drug–target engagement

Layer 1 · target relevance in the cancer

D = Σ w·(−z) / Σ w dependency A = f − median(f) recurrent activation O = NES, or log2 FC vs normal over-activity T = mean(D, A, O), percentiles

Integrates functional dependency, genomic activation and over-activity into a single cancer-specific measure of target relevance.

Layer 2 · drug–target engagement

r = 10^(p − p_best) relative affinity E = 50 + r · (T − 50) engaged relevance S = mean of E over targets Layer 3: percentile of S within the cancer

Credits each target in proportion to binding affinity, so weakly engaged targets contribute little regardless of their biological relevance.

  • Affinity weighting is retained on mechanistic grounds; held-out concordance was unchanged by its inclusion (0.64 vs 0.66, Hub; 0.62 vs 0.61, ChEMBL).
  • Estimated target occupancy at unbound peak plasma concentration, Cmax·fu / (Cmax·fu + Kd), is reported for 86 drugs (24 below 50%) as supporting pharmacological evidence.
7 · Biological validity

Endocrine therapies segregate correctly by breast cancer subtype

Median within-cancer percentile of fulvestrant, tamoxifen and raloxifene. The three-layered objective reverses the ordering produced by signature reversal, consistent with estrogen-receptor dependency and activity in luminal tumours.

8 · Recall by cancer

About one third of approved drugs fall in the top quarter of each ranking

  • Across 41 cancers and 468 approved cancer–drug pairs (ChEMBL), 36% rank in the top quarter (expected by chance: 25%) and 13% in the top 10; the Hub reference gives 36% and 18%.
  • Recall is highest where targeted agents act on dominant drivers or dependencies (head and neck, melanoma, thyroid, bladder, clear-cell kidney) and lowest where approvals rest on minority alterations or few agents (lung, glioblastoma, pheochromocytoma).
  • Missed approvals are mostly excluded by the typical-tumour criterion (e.g. ALK inhibitors in lung adenocarcinoma, PARP and PI3K inhibitors in breast), ranked just outside the top 10 (EGFR inhibitors in lung adenocarcinoma, ranks 17–19), or approved for supportive indications (e.g. amifostine).

9 · Essential targets

Cytotoxic agents are characterised by proliferation, weighted by target coupling

  • Targets required by all proliferating cells (e.g. tubulin, topoisomerase II) carry no cancer-specific dependency signal, and approvals of the corresponding cytotoxic agents instead track the proliferative rate of the cancer (between-cancer AUC 0.63 / 0.57).
  • Scoring these targets by a PAM50-derived proliferation index raised overall concordance to 0.67 / 0.65 and improved between-cancer discrimination for these agents from 0.51 / 0.46 to 0.60 / 0.60.
  • Because proliferation is a property of the cancer, an unweighted index assigns every such agent the same score and credits targets that are essential only in cell culture (HMGCR, the statin target; PPIA, the cyclosporine target).
  • Weighting by each target’s within-tumour correlation with proliferation (ρ: TOP2A 0.79, RRM2 0.77, HMGCR 0.29) preserves the signal for cytotoxic targets, removes statins and cyclosporine from every top 10, and separates agents with different targets.
T = 50 + ρ · (P − 50) P = cancer’s proliferation percentile (PAM50 index), ρ = target’s coupling to proliferation

Remaining limitations: agents sharing a target still share a score (e.g. glucocorticoids), alterations carried by a minority of tumours inflate some targets (e.g. EGFR in lung adenocarcinoma), and cell-line sensitivity data (PRISM, GDSC) do not yet provide independent support.
