Principal conclusions

  • Target-relevance rankings distinguish selected molecular subgroups, but do not establish a drug's mechanism or likely therapeutic benefit.
  • Signed mechanism scores are sensitive to regulator dependence, broad proliferation programs and missing target-specific observations.
  • Mechanism traces retain numerical measurements at each link while making weak, broken and unevaluable links explicit.

Interpretive conventions

“Criterion not met” denotes a completed experiment that failed its predefined requirement.

“Feasibility audit” denotes data coverage without a substantive target-recovery or concordance result.

“Prediction frozen” denotes predicted directions committed before the corresponding response was examined.

Data suitability

Are the disease and perturbation datasets comparable?

D76 · Data adequacy·October 2026
Implemented

Cohort–cell-line compatibility

  • Question. Determine whether a drug perturbation was measured in a cellular context representative of the disease.
  • Finding. The pipeline distinguishes matched, borrowed-only and inadequate evidence; MCF7 does not substitute for basal-like breast tumors.
  • Inference. Carry data-adequacy and source-line identity into every downstream interpretation.

D77 · Cross-line transfer·October 2026
Measured

Cross-cell-line transfer

  • Question. Measure the reproducibility of compound-induced gene expression across cell lines.
  • Finding. Leave-one-cell-line-out mean-profile transfer reached Pearson r ≈ 0.485; the within-line replicate reference was ≈ 0.553.
  • Inference. Borrowed profiles are partial measurements of transferability, not direct observations of a patient tumor.

D120–D124 · Target and lead scores·November 2026
Implemented

Target relevance and pharmacology

  • Question. Assess cancer target dependence, alteration and overactivity alongside drug action annotations.
  • Finding. D120–D124 combined dependency, alteration, regulator activity, target units and relative ChEMBL potency; the production score also contains proliferation handling.
  • Inference. Target relevance, biochemical binding and effective cellular engagement are separate propositions.

D127 · Biomarker benchmark·November 2026
Benchmark passed

Known biomarker comparisons

  • Question. Test whether annotated drugs receive higher ranks in biomarker-relevant disease contexts.
  • Finding. The predefined D127 set passed: 36/49 expected orderings (73.5%); median gap 15.1 percentile points; sign-test p = 0.0007.
  • Inference. This establishes discrimination within a selected benchmark, not causal target engagement or therapeutic benefit.

Measurement validity

Are apparent effects robust to dependence and assay limitations?

D129 · Gene quality·16 Nov 2026
Measured

Quality of inferred genes

  • Question. Separate reconstruction accuracy from the special case of measuring a perturbed target itself.
  • Finding. Inferred genes had median held-out R² = 0.875. An inferred target transcript is not valid evidence that its own genetic perturbation occurred.
  • Inference. Retain inferred downstream genes; require directly measured target mRNA for target-self perturbation QC.

D130 · Correlated nulls·16 Nov 2026
Reinterpreted

Correlation-preserving statistical nulls

  • Question. Determine whether gene-wise permutations exaggerate evidence by ignoring correlated transcriptional responses.
  • Finding. A representative ESR1 sign-null z decreased from 2.81 to 1.09 when correlated structure was preserved; observed M was unchanged.
  • Inference. Null distributions must preserve gene–gene dependence; previously reported significance cannot be retained.

D131 + D133 · Variance structure·16–17 Nov 2026
Reinterpreted

Effective variance and score calibration

  • Question. Test whether large activity vectors provide independent evidence and whether reversal scale is calibrated.
  • Finding. Approximately 1,052 regulators behaved as ≈10 effective variance terms; the reversal scale was inflated ≈10-fold, while the tested within-cohort rankings remained unchanged.
  • Inference. Report dependence, dominant factors and uncertainty rather than interpreting score magnitude as certainty.

D134 S0 · Target retrieval·17 Nov 2026
Feasibility audit

Target recoverability feasibility

  • Question. Identify targets for which distinct compounds can be compared within the same cell line.
  • Finding. The feasibility audit retained 71 target units across 229 compounds and 37 lines; 111 of 193 annotated units had only one compound.
  • Inference. Target-retrieval AUROC remains unmeasured; targets outside the testable set are untested, not undetectable.

D135 S0 · Genetic concordance·17 Nov 2026
Feasibility audit

Drug–genetic perturbation feasibility

  • Question. Determine where a drug profile can be compared with knockout of its own annotated target.
  • Finding. The CRISPR overlap comprised 2,350 drug × target × line cells over seven lines, but cleaner subsets are substantially smaller.
  • Inference. Strength, target expression, essentiality and polypharmacology must be examined before interpreting concordance.

Dose-response methodology

Research phase · 3 experiments or milestones

Mechanism traces

Can evidence be represented as a falsifiable chain?

TRACE · Seven-link procedure·17 Nov 2026
Design only

Seven-link evidence procedure

  • Question. Define a mechanistic claim as a forward-constructed record whose individual links can fail.
  • Finding. Each link records expected and observed behavior, evidence provenance, independence, alternatives and a verdict.
  • Inference. Report the first broken or unevaluable link rather than averaging all links into a suitability score.
