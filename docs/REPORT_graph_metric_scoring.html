D125 · D126 · pre-registered spec · closed 10 Nov 2026

A scoring function that put drugs and diseases in one shared space

The proposal was to stop scoring drugs against diseases regulator-by-regulator and instead score them inside a geometry built from the regulatory network itself — so that two regulators controlling the same genes would stop counting twice. Both halves of the spec were run against their own pre-registered bars. Both failed, and nothing in the production pipeline changed.

Part A — best gain +0.17 pts, bar was +1.0 Part B — known-failure control fired on 0 of 22 Production ranking unchanged
The idea

What was proposed, in plain language

  • Today's score is a dot product. A disease has a vector of regulator activities (which transcription factors are switched on in the tumour). A drug has one too (which it pushes up or down). The current score, called reversal, is essentially: how much does the drug's vector point opposite to the disease's vector.
  • The flaw it was meant to fix: regulators are not independent. E2F1, E2F2, E2F7, E2F8, FOXM1 and MYBL2 all drive roughly the same proliferation genes. A dot product treats them as six separate pieces of evidence. They are closer to one.
  • The fix: change the ruler, not the vectors. Build a graph where two regulators are connected if their target genes overlap. Then measure agreement in a geometry where moving along a heavily-connected cluster counts for less than moving along a lone regulator. That is the "shared representation space" — drug and disease are measured with the same, network-aware ruler.
  • One knob, λ, controls how much shaping happens. Small λ means the network dominates. Large λ means the network is ignored. Crucially, at λ = ∞ the new score becomes exactly the score already in production — so the thing being tested contains today's method as a special case.
  • The second half, Part B, was a different kind of fix. Rather than reweighting, it asked: can we check whether a drug's effect is mechanistically plausible — does a signed path exist from the drug's actual protein target to the regulators it appears to move, with the right sign at the end?
The maths

The scoring function

// the graph-metric reversal score
revL(d, g) = − (dT K g) / √(dT K d)

// the metric, from the regulator-overlap graph
K = (L + λI)−1
Lsym = I − D−1/2 A D−1/2
Aij = |Ti ∩ Tj| / min(|Ti|, |Tj|)  // dropped below 0.1
d
disease regulator-activity vector — the tumour's TF profile
g
drug regulator-activity vector — what the compound moves
A
overlap between two regulators' target gene sets (Szymkiewicz–Simpson); edges under 0.1 dropped, no self-loops, direction of regulation ignored
L
symmetric normalised Laplacian — the graph's "roughness" operator
K
the metric. As λ grows, K → I/λ and the score collapses back to ordinary reversal
λ
the single knob: 0.1 (network dominates) through ∞ (network ignored)
Why the λ = ∞ nesting mattered

Because production is a special case of the proposal, the test needed no separate baseline and no re-implementation. Running the sweep down from ∞ asks one question with no wiggle room: does shaping the space by the network ever beat not shaping it? A reimplementation that merely matched production would have left room to argue about the comparison. This left none.

Part A result

Shaping the space made the score worse

The benchmark: 1,774 LINCS experiments where one transcription factor was knocked down or overexpressed on purpose. A score is correct when it moves that factor in the expected direction. The bar, fixed in advance, was a 1.0-point gain over production.

0.6077production correct rate (λ = ∞)
+0.17best gain, in points, at λ = 2
1.0points needed, set before the run
−3.5points at λ = 0.1, the most network shaping
  • Monotone in the wrong direction. The score improves as the graph is turned off. Three of the seven λ values are worse than production; the best is inside noise.
  • For CollecTRI, the optimum is production itself — λ = 8 and λ = ∞ give the identical 0.6220. There was no interior best to find.
  • Per transcription factor it is a wash. Of 131 factors with at least 6 signatures: 18 better, 16 worse, 97 unchanged. Median change 0.000.
  • The graphs themselves were healthy — 42 of them, 472–1,266 regulators each, median mean-degree 50.2, 0–11 isolated nodes. The redundancy the method targets is real and measurable.
  • So the conclusion is not "the graph was broken". Down-weighting redundant regulators removes more signal than noise. The duplicated proliferation factors carry information, not just repetition.
Part B

The mechanism filter, and the prior it ran on

  • The question it asked. For each drug, take its real protein target and walk outward through a network of signed, directed signalling edges. If a regulator the drug appears to move is reachable, the path's signs predict which way it should move. Compare prediction against what the data says.
  • What it would have bought. Not a better ranking — a flag. Drugs whose apparent effect has no mechanistic story would be marked, never silently rescored.
  • The prior was built from scratch and pinned. OmniPath has no data release number, so the cached files are the dependency: both tables are checksummed and registered, with a note that re-pulling is a new decision rather than a refresh.
Three things the build corrected about the written spec

1. The field the spec named, consensus_sign, does not exist in OmniPath; the real fields are consensus_stimulation and consensus_inhibition, so "signed" was implemented as exactly one of them being true. 2. The literature-support clause drops 54,598 edges against roughly 15,000 each for direction and sign — support, not sign, is what defines this prior. 3. SIGNOR alone keeps 63% of the edges and 79% of the nodes, so the spec's coverage argument for preferring OmniPath is weaker than it claimed.

Part B result

The filter endorsed the exact failure it was built to catch

The decisive test, written into the spec in advance: anti-oestrogens in the breast cohorts are a known misread of the pipeline. If the mechanism flag does not fire there, the filter does not work and D126 closes. It fired on none of them.

The cause, and why it matters beyond this experiment

The filter examines each drug's 25 largest activity shifts. For anti-oestrogens in breast cohorts those 25 are the proliferation block — E2F7, E2F8, ZNF367, CENPA, E2F1, FOXM1, MXD3, PA2G4, E2F2 — because that is what dominates the signal. ESR1 never makes the list, so the oestrogen axis is never tested. And an ESR1 antagonist genuinely does explain lowering FOXM1 and MYC, so every path checks out and the drug passes.

This is the same proliferation dominance found earlier in D80 and D85, reappearing inside the diagnostic built to audit it. The check was aimed at growth arrest, not at mechanism.

Two notes on what did not happen. The λ = 2 result of +0.17 points was not rounded up, re-weighted, or re-run on a different read; the +1.0 bar was written and committed before the sweep started. And when the CARNIVAL arm turned out to need a MILP solver that is not installed, it was recorded as not executable rather than quietly replaced with the heuristic it was supposed to license.

What it means

Reading the result

  • The shared representation space did not beat reversal. It is a clean negative: production is nested inside the proposal, so the comparison has no implementation gap to argue about, and the correct rate went the wrong way at every setting but one.
  • This is the third attempt of its kind to fail, after the pnet work (D86) and the latent-factor attempts. The pattern is consistent: regulator redundancy is genuinely there, and removing it costs more than it saves.
  • Part B failed for a more interesting reason than Part A. Not "the idea is wrong" but "the thing being inspected was chosen badly". Selecting a drug's 25 biggest shifts guarantees you inspect proliferation, whatever the drug does.
  • Nothing reached production. The flag was reported-only by design and is not emitted; the live drug ranking is byte-for-byte what it was. With Part A closed at A1 and Part B closed at B3, the whole specification is now resolved and none of it changed the pipeline.
  • If D126 were revived, one thing has to change first: pick target-proximal regulators — the ones actually downstream of the drug's protein — instead of the 25 largest shifts. That is a different test and would need its own rule fixed in advance, plus its own control. The 46% of drug targets with no outgoing signed edge at all would still cap how far it can go.
