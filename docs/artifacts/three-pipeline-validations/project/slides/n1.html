Measurement ceiling, then similarity at matched activity

Ceiling

0.33 — 2 conditions

0.57 — 4 to 5 conditions

0.79 — 10 or more conditions

Median R = 0.53 overall; 0.38 early, 0.59 late. The ceiling is governed by the number of conditions behind a drug, not by any property of the method.

Approximately 45% of the reproducible gene-level pattern is not determined by the activity value.

On why the ceiling comes first: a between-drug similarity judged against 1.0 would be misleading, because a single drug's own gene vector does not reproduce at 1.0 either. The ceiling is the appropriate denominator, and computing it before the comparison was specified in advance so that it could not be chosen to suit the result. On the ceiling's dependence on sample size: the progression from 0.33 at two conditions to 0.79 at ten or more indicates that the limitation is data volume rather than the decomposition. It also explains the early-late difference — early cells have a median of three conditions against five for late — and means gene-level quantities are most trustworthy in the late window and in well-sampled compounds. On the registered status of this test: it failed as pre-registered, for two reasons that were recorded rather than corrected quietly. One criterion was partly tautological, because activity is the sum of the gene contributions and opposite-sign activity therefore forces opposing vectors. The specification also pooled early and late windows whose reliabilities differ materially. Both defects are documented, and the corrected analysis appears on the next slide. If asked whether 0.30 against 0.53 is a large gap: expressed as a fraction of the attainable ceiling it is 0.57, so roughly 43 to 45 per cent of reproducible structure is unexplained. That is the quantity to quote rather than the raw cosine.
