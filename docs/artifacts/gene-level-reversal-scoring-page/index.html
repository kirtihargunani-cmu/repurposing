Gene-Level Reversal Scoring

Drug repurposing · week of 17–22 Nov 2026

One NES score is not one mechanism

The reversal score multiplies totals: how dysregulated a regulator is, times how much a drug moves it. It never asks whether the same genes are involved. This week we measured what that costs — and found the score has been rewarding chemotherapy.

1

The master-regulator score is lossy, and we can say by how much

  • A drug's gene-level fingerprint inside one regulon, compared against itself (split conditions in half): cosine 0.53. That is the measurement ceiling — nothing can match a vector better than it matches itself.
  • Two different drugs with near-identical activity on that regulator: 0.30.
  • So roughly 45% of the reproducible gene pattern is not determined by the NES. Among drugs moving a regulator the same direction by the same amount, activity predicts essentially none of the residual (Spearman +0.0001, late window).
  • The ceiling is a sample-size effect, not a flaw: 0.33 at 2 conditions rising to 0.79 at 10+.
Registered as a failure. The pre-registered version of this test failed, for two reasons we recorded rather than quietly fixed: one criterion was partly tautological, and it pooled early and late windows whose reliability differs (0.38 vs 0.59). Corrected and split by window, it holds in late and is inconclusive in early.
2

The networks hold real mechanism, not co-expression noise

  • Regulon membership is context, not estimation error: the same-cohort noise floor is Jaccard 0.552 while cross-tissue is 0.029. The same transcription factor is genuinely wired to different genes in different diseases.
  • Against LINCS knockdown signatures — the one test that does not depend on our pipeline being right — the ARACNe TF set reads knockdowns at AUC 0.55 (shRNA) and 0.63 (CRISPR), passing 38 of 175 testable TFs.
  • The decisive case. ARACNe's ESR1 regulon reads an ESR1 knockdown correctly. So when anti-estrogens read as ESR1 activation, that is a real drug effect — GATA3 and FOXA1 rise under the drugs — not a broken network. The benchmark was too thin for per-TF choices (1–5 signatures per TF, ARACNe vs CollecTRI paired p = 0.6), so it is reported and never used to pick regulons.
  • VIPER's activity is exactly reconstructable from its gene-level terms: max |difference| 4.8 × 10⁻⁷ against the stored values, across 21,588 drug × cohort × window cells. The nested breakdown is the same arithmetic, not an approximation of it.
  • Independent confirmation from clustering: regulator programs found across patients and across drugs — two unrelated axes — recover the same hubs (PRRX1↔PRRX1 Jaccard 0.71, DMTF1↔DMTF1 0.61). The endothelial program found in luminal breast contains three of the five regulons that most oppose fulvestrant.
3

Timepoint is a real dimension, and it cannot be imputed

  • At 6 h a drug's response is genuinely smaller, so a single activity floor discards 76% of early signatures. A per-timepoint floor (0.25 at 6 h) raises active early signatures from 4,938 to 10,889.
  • The windows disagree, and the disagreement is biology: 37% of regulator terms flip sign between early and late. For anti-estrogens in MCF7 the ESR1 programme reads −0.056 at 6 h (correct) and +0.094 at 24 h (inverted) — and within that line the inverted window outvotes the correct one roughly 10 : 1 in a pooled average.
  • We asked whether the missing window can be predicted, and pre-registered the answer either way. It cannot. Split-half reliability is 0.679 early / 0.795 late, giving attainable ceilings of 0.824 / 0.892. The best model reaches Spearman 0.380 — against 0.382 for simply copying the other window. Sign agreement 0.62–0.63 against a 0.75 bar. Verdict: CLOSED, in both directions.
  • About 80% of a window's reliable variance is absent from the other, and the discordance does not shrink with more conditions (38.8% → 35.8%). It is not noise waiting for more data.
  • Nor is it a property of the drug: a drug's behaviour in its other cohorts predicts this one at Spearman −0.05, and within-drug spread (7.90) is smaller than between-drug (12.88). "Drug X is early-acting" is not a statement this data supports.
  • So every score is window-separated, and an unmeasured window is labelled unknown rather than guessed — the empirically correct label, not merely the cautious one.
Coverage is the limit. Only 29 of 41 cohorts have any window data, and 12 have no condition-level profiles in their matched cell lines at all — there, unknown is permanent, not pending.

5

What the gene level actually does: it demotes chemotherapy

  • Classic cytotoxics — anthracyclines, topoisomerase inhibitors, antimetabolites, platins, taxanes, vincas — move −9.2 percentile points. Everything else moves +2.6. Mann-Whitney p = 5.4 × 10⁻²².
  • Demoted: daunorubicin, doxorubicin, epirubicin, topotecan, cytarabine, and the whole mTOR family. Promoted: azacitidine (41 cohorts, +52 points), bortezomib and ixazomib, cardiac glycosides, retinoids, corticosteroids.
  • Promoted drugs have mean target dependency 71.3 against 59.2 for demoted ones.
A score that moves away from cytotoxics and toward drugs whose targets are genuinely depended on is doing what a repurposing pipeline is supposed to do.
6

Why this looked like a failure for most of the week

  • We had been judging every score on approval recovery — do drugs already approved for this cancer rank high. Measured properly, that yardstick is bimodal: 9 of 41 cohorts score significantly above chance and 9 significantly below (endometrial at z = −7.7).
  • In the failing cohorts the approved sets are largely cytotoxic, and a single generic proliferation axis beats the mechanism score there.
  • So a score that demotes chemotherapy must lose approval AUC while gaining on target biology. The null result and the positive result are the same fact measured two ways.
The methodological takeaway. Approval recovery is the wrong instrument for a mechanistic claim, and we can now show why rather than assert it. Biomarker pairs and target-role components are the right anchors.
7

What else held up, and what did not

  • Held. Top-ranked novel drugs converge on the mechanism profile of drugs already approved for that disease — effect +0.277 early, +0.312 late, every cell positive — and it survives restricting to drugs sharing no annotated target or MOA class with the approved set (+0.263 / +0.312). Convergence by a different route.
  • Did not hold. Collapsing 800+ regulators into 20–40 programs does not improve ranking — the collapse is an orthonormal rotation, and truncation gave no gain. Programs stay for interpretation, not scoring.
  • Did not hold. Cross-cohort transfer. With identical cell lines but different networks, the same drug's regulator activity correlates only +0.667; cross-tissue +0.330. Regulon content overlaps at Jaccard 0.06–0.09 even between two breast subtypes.
8

Open, and honest about it

  • Four scoring attempts returned null, and two were implementation errors of mine, not tests of the idea — a double-counted sign, and a term scaled 100× too small. Both were found by decomposition after the result looked wrong, and both are recorded.
  • The composition that should work — reversal at full weight plus the covariance weighted by its measured reliability, scale-free — is written but not yet tested. It needs its own registration.
  • The pooled per-drug association is small (Spearman +0.089 with dependency). The per-cohort gains are much larger. Liver moves the wrong way in both windows.
  • Several top movers are implausible low-count drugs with no target annotation. The pattern is in the aggregate, not in any single name.
Every figure here is from a committed run with its own pre-registration or recorded amendment. Nothing was tuned after a number was seen; where a threshold failed, the failure is in the record alongside the corrected analysis.
